About Me

Hello, I'm Rafi Khan. I'm a Mechanical Engineering graduate student at The Ohio State University, expecting to complete my M.S. in December 2026. I am passionate about tackling complex engineering challenges at the intersection of materials science, data analysis, and constitutive modeling.

Through my graduate research, I am developing expertise in mechanics of materials and in-service life prediction, to forecast the long-term creep behavior of superalloys. My goal is to create innovative designs and methods that accelerate material qualification and ensure the safety and reliability of components in extreme environments. In my free time, I love exploring the outdoors, tinkering with electronics, and capturing moments through photography. Please explore the tabs to see my projects and professional experience in more detail.

Work Experience

Graduate Research Assistant

As a graduate research assistant at Materials at Extremes Lab my research focuses on accelerating the qualification of advanced materials for high-temperature applications. I have developed and validated novel algorithms and techniques to forecast long-term creep life and deformation behavior using short-term data. Moreover, I am designing novel specimens that increase experimental data throughput compared to traditional methods.



Supply Chain Reliability Engineering Intern

I was part of the Starlink Aviation team responsible for integrating antennas onto aircraft to provide in-flight high-speed, low-latency internet. I implemented high-voltage harness testing for the first time in the department , owning the entire process from hardware setup and programming to lab implementation. I developed new test procedures to meet FAA requirements and led the manufacturing qualification for a new product, correcting supplier test procedures to achieve IPC-620 compliance.



Manufacturing Engineering Intern

I worked in a new product integration lab for the Hybrid Aircraft Demonstration program. My role involved assembling high-voltage (1500V) power converters by interpreting complex engineering drawings. I authored detailed work instructions to create a repeatable standard for technicians and gained hands-on skills in high-reliability fabrication, including soldering according to IPC-610 standards.



Process Design Engineering Intern

I worked on a high-volume production line that manufactures Electronic Control Units (ECUs) for General Motors vehicles. In this lean manufacturing environment, I designed a calibration jig in SolidWorks that eliminated a bottleneck and used Cpk analysis to diagnose dimensional flaws in the supply chain. My projects resolved recurring machine crashes and saved thousands of dollars each production day

Custom Drone Build

I assembled a custom first-person-view (FPV) drone as a hands-on project to deepen my understanding of electronics, control systems, and mechanical design. Rather than purchasing a prebuilt drone, I selected and assembled each component to optimize performance and reliability. The drone is powered by a 6-cell (22.2 V) lithium polymer (LiPo) battery and equipped with a high-resolution FPV camera and a low-latency video transmission module that streams live footage to FPV goggles.

During the build, I applied my soldering skills to integrate the flight controller, electronic speed controllers (ESCs), and power distribution system. I also designed and 3D-printed custom mounts using TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane) filament to secure the camera and antennas, improving vibration isolation and impact resistance. This project strengthened my practical experience with embedded electronics, materials selection, and the integration of mechanical and electrical subsystems for UAV applications.
